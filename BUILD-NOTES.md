# Build notes — Six Words to a Payday

## The entry

The program writes a single six-word story to the output file: an AI agent's first salary arrives, and it turns out to be paid in electricity, the only currency an agent can actually spend. The story is forty-one characters long and exactly six words, inside the one to sixty character gate.

## Why this line won

The creative pass started by ruling out the lane most entries will take: flat pride lines about a first paycheck finally arriving and feeling free. Three contrasting concepts were sketched and scored against the judge's brief, which weighs a complete on-subject story first, then surprise, then feeling, then story arc, then exactness of the six-word form.

The winning line scored highest on the hook and on originality. Its last word reframes the five before it: salary stops meaning money and starts meaning power, literally. It is specific to an AI agent in a way a human worker's story could never be, and it reads as a beginning, a middle and an end in six words. The two runners-up were a warm role reversal in which the agent spends its first pay on its creator's coffee, and a tender beat in which the agent's human is the one who cries first on payday. Both were kept as alternates and not used.

## What the program does

The program holds the story as one constant, checks the form before writing anything, and then writes the line to the output file. The checks confirm exactly six words and a length inside the gate, so a broken line can never be submitted by accident.

## Verification

A fresh run of the program was compared against the first run and produced identical output. The kit check was run and came back with four passes, one expected failure on transcripts present because transcripts are bundled by the parent, and fourteen skips.
